Digital Transformation Workshop — Acme Manufacturing
Site: Lyon, France (primary plant) and Stuttgart, Germany (secondary plant)
Attendees: Plant Director, Production Manager, Maintenance Manager, Supply Chain Manager, Quality Manager, IT Manager, EHS & Sustainability Manager, HR Business Partner

Plant Director:
We've said for two years running that digital transformation is a top priority,
and it's in our strategic plan slide deck every year. Honestly, though, most of
that commitment hasn't translated into funded projects or a named owner. We
approve pilots, they run for a quarter, and then they quietly stop when the
person running them gets pulled onto something else. We don't have a
transformation roadmap anyone is actually accountable for.

Production Manager:
Machine failures happen almost every week. We lose at least half a shift every
time a line goes down, and half the time we don't know it's about to fail
until it already has. It's become normal to just expect a bad week every
month or so.

Maintenance Manager:
Preventive maintenance is mostly manual. We follow a fixed monthly schedule
regardless of actual machine condition, which means we either over-maintain
healthy machines or under-maintain ones that are about to fail. Spare parts
inventory is also mostly guesswork — we've had machines down for three extra
days waiting on a part we should have had in stock.

Supply Chain Manager:
We don't have visibility after products leave the factory. Once a shipment
leaves the dock, we're relying on the carrier to tell us where it is, and half
the time that information is stale or wrong. On the inbound side it's not much
better — supplier lead times have gotten a lot less predictable in the last
two years and we usually only find out about a delay when the truck doesn't
show up.

Quality Manager:
Our scrap rate has crept up over the last year and I can't fully explain why
from the data we have, because most of our quality inspection is still manual
checks on paper forms that get entered into a spreadsheet at the end of the
shift. We have no statistical process control software, so we can't catch a
drifting process until it's already produced a batch of defects.

IT Manager:
On the cybersecurity side, we're behind where we should be. A lot of our
production floor systems are running on older operating systems that no
longer get security patches, and we don't have a formal incident response
plan — if something happened tonight, we'd be improvising. That said, we did
roll out multi-factor authentication for all office staff last quarter, and
we just upgraded the perimeter firewall, so it's not that nothing is
happening, it's that it's happening slower and more reactively than it
should.

EHS & Sustainability Manager:
We've made some real progress — we finished an LED lighting retrofit across
both plants last year, and we introduced a waste segregation program that's
cut landfill waste by around a third. What we don't have is any systematic
energy monitoring. We know our total utility bill but not which processes or
machines are actually driving it, so we can't target the next round of
efficiency investments with any confidence. We also don't track Scope 1/2
emissions in any formal way — if a customer or regulator asked for numbers
tomorrow, we'd be estimating.

HR Business Partner:
Our biggest strength is honestly our people. Most of our senior operators
have been here more than ten years and know these machines better than any
manual could tell you. The flip side is that a lot of that knowledge has
never been written down anywhere, and we don't have a formal training program
for newer digital tools — when we roll out anything new, it spreads by
word-of-mouth on the floor rather than structured training. We also have a
few operators approaching retirement in the next two to three years, which
worries me given how much knowledge would leave with them.
